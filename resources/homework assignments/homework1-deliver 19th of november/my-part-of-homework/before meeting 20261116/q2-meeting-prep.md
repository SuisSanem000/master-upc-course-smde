## 2. Part (a): Distributions by Age Group (`age_cat`) and Performance Class (`class`)

- **What was checked**: The distributions of `body_fat_pct`, `diastolic`, `sit_ups`, and `broad_jump_cm` across the 4 age groups (`<30`, `30-39`, `40-49`, `50+`) and the 4 performance classes (`D`, `C`, `B`, `A`).
- **Tables & Figures**: `q2a_summary_age`, `q2a_summary_class`, `q2a_boxplots_age`, `q2a_boxplots_class`.
- **Key Findings by Age Group (`age_cat`)**:
  - **`body_fat_pct`**: Increases steadily with age, from a mean of **21.60%** in `<30` to **25.93%** in `50+` (especially from age 40 upward).
  - **`diastolic`**: Increases slightly from **77.47 mmHg** in `<30` to **81.82 mmHg** in `40-49`, and stays almost flat in `50+` (**81.02 mmHg**).
  - **`sit_ups`**: Decreases strongly with age, from **46.39** in `<30` down to **27.35** in `50+`.
  - **`broad_jump_cm`**: Almost identical in `<30` (**203.15 cm**) and `30-39` (**204.38 cm**), then drops sharply in `40-49` (**187.65 cm**) and `50+` (**160.19 cm**).
- **Key Findings by Performance Class (`class`, from `D` = worst to `A` = best)**:
  - **`body_fat_pct`**: Drops from **26.88%** in class `D` to **20.51%** in class `A` (with the biggest jump between `D` and `C`).
  - **`diastolic`**: Nearly the same across all classes (only changes from **80.57 mmHg** in `D` to **78.66 mmHg** in `A`).
  - **`sit_ups`**: Rises strongly with better performance class, from **30.67** in `D` to **47.91** in `A`.
  - **`broad_jump_cm`**: Rises steadily from **176.56 cm** in `D` to **203.01 cm** in `A`.
- **Spread & Skewness**: Within each group, the mean and median are very close, so the distributions are fairly symmetric. Standard deviations are similar across groups (the largest-to-smallest variance ratio is at most **1.86**, for `sit_ups` by `class`, which is below the slide rule-of-thumb threshold of 2).

## 3. Part (b): One-Way ANOVA & Three Post-Hoc Tests

### 3.1 Outliers
- No extra outliers were removed in Q2b because Q1b already removed all biologically impossible values and values outside adult health reference ranges; the remaining extreme points are realistic values.

### 3.2 One-Way ANOVA Results (`q2b_anova` — 8 Models)
- **All 8 one-way ANOVA models reject $H_0$** (equal group means) at the 95% confidence level ($p < 10^{-16}$ for 7 models, and $p = 2.58 \times 10^{-13}$ for `diastolic ~ class`).
- Because sample size is very large ($n = 12,742$), even tiny differences are statistically significant, so we also look at **$\eta^2$ (`Eta_sq`, share of variance explained)**:
  - **Strong effects**:
    - `sit_ups ~ age_cat`: $F = 1823.9$, $\eta^2 = 0.300$ (**30.0%** of variance explained).
    - `broad_jump_cm ~ age_cat`: $F = 1128.7$, $\eta^2 = 0.210$ (**21.0%**).
    - `sit_ups ~ class`: $F = 1044.2$, $\eta^2 = 0.197$ (**19.7%**).
  - **Moderate effects**:
    - `body_fat_pct ~ class`: $F = 547.6$, $\eta^2 = 0.114$ (**11.4%**).
    - `body_fat_pct ~ age_cat`: $F = 291.8$, $\eta^2 = 0.064$ (**6.4%**).
    - `broad_jump_cm ~ class`: $F = 274.0$, $\eta^2 = 0.061$ (**6.1%**).
  - **Very small / negligible effects**:
    - `diastolic ~ age_cat`: $F = 141.5$, $\eta^2 = 0.032$ (**3.2%**).
    - `diastolic ~ class`: $F = 20.6$, $\eta^2 = 0.005$ (**0.5%** — practically negligible).

### 3.3 Assumption Checks (`q2b_assumptions` & `q2b_qqplots`)
1. **Normality of residuals**: Shapiro-Wilk (on 5,000 residuals) rejects normality in all 8 models ($p \le 2.87 \times 10^{-10} < 0.05$), but the Q-Q plots (`q2b_qqplots`) follow the straight line closely except for slight tail deviations, and with $n = 12,742$ ANOVA is robust to slight non-normality.
2. **Equal variances (homoscedasticity)**:
   - Levene's test rejects equal variances in 7 of the 8 models ($p < 0.05$), and only does not reject it for `diastolic ~ class` ($p = 0.0618 > 0.05$).
   - Breusch-Pagan rejects equal variances in 7 of the 8 models, and only does not reject it for `broad_jump_cm ~ age_cat` ($p = 0.199 > 0.05$).
   - However, the ratio of largest to smallest group variance (`Var_ratio`) is between **1.06 and 1.86** (all below the rule-of-thumb limit of 2 from the slides), meaning the formal tests reject homoscedasticity mainly due to the very large sample size.
3. **Independence of errors**: Durbin-Watson does not reject independence ($p > 0.05$) for 7 of the 8 models, and barely rejects it for `sit_ups ~ age_cat` ($p = 0.0201$).

### 3.4 Three Post-Hoc Tests Comparison (`q2b_posthoc_age`, `q2b_posthoc_class`, `q2b_posthoc_summary`)
- We compared **Tukey HSD**, **Bonferroni**, and **Fisher's LSD** across all $\binom{4}{2} = 6$ pairs for each of the 8 models:
  - **All 6 pairs differ significantly in all 3 tests** for:
    - `diastolic ~ age_cat` and `sit_ups ~ age_cat`
    - `body_fat_pct ~ class`, `sit_ups ~ class`, and `broad_jump_cm ~ class`
  - **Where the 3 tests agree that a pair does NOT differ**:
    - `broad_jump_cm ~ age_cat`: **5 of 6 pairs differ** in all three tests; the pair `30-39 vs <30` (difference $+1.23$ cm) is **not** significant in any test (Tukey $p = 0.450$, Bonferroni $p = 0.834$, LSD $p = 0.139$).
    - `diastolic ~ class`: The pair `B vs C` (difference $+0.08$ mmHg) is **not** significant in any test ($p \ge 0.745$).
  - **Where LSD differs from Tukey HSD and Bonferroni**:
    - Because **LSD applies no multiple-comparison penalty**, its p-values are always the smallest, **Bonferroni** is the most conservative (largest p-values), and **Tukey HSD** lies in between.
    - This changes the decision at $\alpha = 0.05$ in two borderline pairs:
      1. **`body_fat_pct ~ age_cat`, pair `30-39 vs <30`** (difference $+0.36\%$): Significant under **LSD** ($p = 0.0246 < 0.05$), but **not significant** under **Tukey HSD** ($p = 0.111$) or **Bonferroni** ($p = 0.148$).
      2. **`diastolic ~ class`, pair `A vs C`** (difference $-0.59$ mmHg): Significant under **LSD** ($p = 0.0157 < 0.05$), but **not significant** under **Tukey HSD** ($p = 0.0741$) or **Bonferroni** ($p = 0.0941$).

## 4. Part (c): Two-Way ANOVA (`age_cat * gender` on `sit_ups` and `broad_jump_cm`)

### 4.1 Type II Two-Way ANOVA & Interaction (`q2c_anova` & `q2c_interaction`)
- Because group sizes are unbalanced and `age_cat` and `gender` are associated (from Q1f), `R/Q2.R` uses **Type II ANOVA tests** (`car::Anova(model, type = 2)`).
- **Results for `sit_ups`**:
  - `age_cat`: $F = 2178.0, p < 10^{-16}$, partial $\eta^2 = 0.339$.
  - `gender`: $F = 4644.6, p < 10^{-16}$, partial $\eta^2 = 0.267$.
  - **Interaction `age_cat:gender`**: **$F = 23.4, p = 4.17 \times 10^{-15} < 0.05$**, partial $\eta^2 = 0.005$.
- **Results for `broad_jump_cm`**:
  - `age_cat`: $F = 1973.2, p < 10^{-16}$, partial $\eta^2 = 0.317$.
  - `gender`: $F = 17231.9, p < 10^{-16}$, partial $\eta^2 = 0.575$.
  - **Interaction `age_cat:gender`**: **$F = 9.8, p = 1.99 \times 10^{-6} < 0.05$**, partial $\eta^2 = 0.002$.
- **What the Interaction Means (`q2c_interaction` plot & `q2c_posthoc` table)**:
  - For both variables, the interaction is **statistically significant** ($p < 0.05$), though its partial $\eta^2$ is very small ($0.5\%$ and $0.2\%$), so the two gender lines in `q2c_interaction` are nearly parallel except between `<30` and `30-39`.
  - Specifically, from `<30` to `30-39`, **women drop much more than men**:
    - In `sit_ups`, women drop by **$-6.15$** sit-ups (`30-39 vs <30`), whereas men drop by only **$-2.62$** sit-ups.
    - In `broad_jump_cm`, women drop by **$-8.55$ cm** from `<30` to `30-39`, whereas men drop by only **$-2.59$ cm** (which is why in the one-way ANOVA of Q2b, where genders were combined and the `30-39` group had a slightly higher share of men, the overall mean between `<30` and `30-39` appeared flat!).
  - Across all 6 age-group pairs within each gender (`q2c_posthoc`), **all 6 pairs differ significantly** in all three post-hoc tests (Tukey, Bonferroni, LSD) for both women and men.

### 4.2 Assumptions of the Two-Way Models (`q2c_assumptions` & `q2c_qqplots`)
- Shapiro-Wilk, Levene, and Breusch-Pagan reject normality and equal variances ($p < 10^{-11}$), while the Q-Q plots (`q2c_qqplots`) are close to the normal line and the maximum-to-minimum cell variance ratio across the 8 `age_cat x gender` groups is small (**1.43** for `sit_ups` and **1.41** for `broad_jump_cm`, well below 2).
- Durbin-Watson does not reject independence for `broad_jump_cm` ($p = 0.854$) and is borderline for `sit_ups` ($p = 0.0461$).
